## 1. ASIC company name vs business name vs registered trade mark: what rights each gives

### Takeaway
Company names and business names registered with ASIC are administrative identifiers. They give no exclusive or ownership rights to the words, and they do not stop someone else using a similar name. Only a registered trade mark from IP Australia gives an exclusive, enforceable right to use the mark for the listed goods and services across Australia. A domain name licence gives only the domain. Avenris Care Pty Ltd must also register "Avenris Care Support Services" as a business name, because that name differs from its company name.

### Cited Findings
- An ASIC business name registration links a trading name to the entity behind it. ASIC generally blocks identical or nearly identical names, but similar names can still be registered. ASIC's guidance says registration does not stop others using similar words or expressions in their business name — [Brandu Legal](https://www.brandulegal.com.au/business-names-vs-trade-marks-in-australia-why-your-asic-registration-may-not-be-enough-to-protect-your-brand/); [SLK Lawyers](https://www.slklawyers.com.au/business-name-vs-trade-mark-australia-why-asic-registration-does-not-protect-your-brand/); [Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/protecting-your-business-name-trademark-vs-copyright/)
- A registered trade mark gives the exclusive legal right to use the mark for the listed goods and services. It also supports infringement action against identical or confusingly similar marks used for the same or related goods or services — [IP Australia: What are trade marks?](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/trade-marks/what-are-trade-marks); [Trademarkia](https://www.trademarkia.com/news/trademarks/trademark-vs-business-name-australia-difference); [Axe Legal](https://axelegal.com.au/business-names-and-trademarks/)
- A domain registered under auDA gives you only the domain. It does not protect the business name — [Brandu Legal](https://www.brandulegal.com.au/business-names-vs-trade-marks-in-australia-why-your-asic-registration-may-not-be-enough-to-protect-your-brand/)
- ASIC: a company that wants to trade under a name different from its company name must register a business name. A company trading under its exact company name does not need a separate registration. Dropping "Pty Ltd" counts as a different name, so it needs registration — [ASIC: Register a business name](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-business-and-companies/business-names/register-a-business-name); [ASIC: Trading names are not registered business names](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-business-and-companies/business-basics/trading-names-are-not-registered-business-names); [Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/how-to-register-your-business-name-with-asic/)
- Trading under an unregistered business name contravenes the Business Names Registration Act 2011, and ASIC can issue infringement notices — [Sprintlaw: Does a trading name need to be registered?](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/does-a-trading-name-need-to-be-registered/)
- Government fees, 2026-27 (from 1 July 2026, per secondary fee guides):
  - Business name: $47 for 1 year or $108 for 3 years.
  - Pty Ltd company registration: $636 (up from $611).
  - Company annual review: $342.
  - Late lodgement: $102 within one month, $428 after that.
  - Sources: [Mirai Legal ASIC fee guide 2026-27](https://mirailegal.au/guides/asic-fees); [AusBusinessRegister ASIC fees 2026-27](https://ausbusinessregister.com.au/asic-fees-australia/); [AusBusinessRegister annual review 2026](https://ausbusinessregister.com.au/asic-annual-review-guide-2026/)
  - Older pages give lower figures ($42/$98 at Sprintlaw, $45/$105 at Structly). Those appear to be earlier fee years — [Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/asic-business-name-registration-fees-explained/); [Structly](https://structly.com.au/learn/asic-fees-explained)
- A company name registration with ASIC does not give trade mark rights. A trade mark is the way to protect the brand — [Legal500: Should I register my company name before registering it as a trade mark?](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/australia/intellectual-property/should-i-register-my-company-name-before-registering-it-as-a-trade-mark)

### Inferences
- "Avenris Care Pty Ltd" needs no separate business name to trade under that exact name. "Avenris Care Support Services" does need a business name registered to Avenris Care Pty Ltd ($47 for 1 year, $108 for 3 years).
- "Avenris Technologies" and "Avenris Healthcare" can each be registered later as a business name (held by the holding company or by a subsidiary) or as a company name. Registering them early only blocks identical names on the ASIC register. It does not reserve the brand. ASIC also offers a name reservation for a fee, but this research did not verify the current fee.
- The real protection for the shared element is a trade mark for AVENRIS. Ownership is usually best placed in the holding company, which licenses the mark to the operating subsidiaries. That is a common structuring practice, not something found in the sources here, so confirm it with an attorney or accountant. If the holding company is not yet incorporated, filing in the founder's name or Avenris Care Pty Ltd's name and assigning later is possible, but assignment costs and paperwork were not researched.

### Gaps
- Could not read ASIC's own fee table directly. The 2026-27 fees come from secondary guides that agree with each other.
- ASIC company name reservation fee (form 410) was not verified.

## 2. Descriptive words ("care", "healthcare", "support services"): effect on filing AVENRIS vs AVENRIS CARE vs a logo

### Takeaway
Under s 41 of the Trade Marks Act 1995, a mark must be capable of distinguishing the applicant's services. Invented words sit at the top of the distinctiveness scale and descriptive words near the bottom. "AVENRIS" looks like a coined word, so it should be inherently strong (subject to a search for prior similar marks). "CARE", "HEALTHCARE", "SUPPORT SERVICES" and "TECHNOLOGIES" are descriptive of the services and give no protection on their own. In a combined mark, the examiner assesses the whole mark and AVENRIS carries the distinctiveness. A plain word mark AVENRIS gives the broadest and most flexible protection.

### Cited Findings
- IP Australia's examination manual: an application must be rejected if the mark is not capable of distinguishing the applicant's goods or services from those of others (s 41) — [IP Australia Manual 22.1](http://manuals.ipaustralia.gov.au/trademark/1.-registrability-under-section-41-of-the-trade-marks-act-1995)
- Invented or coined words have "very high" inherent distinctiveness. Descriptive words rate low to none. Descriptive terms are best combined with an invented word or a distinctive logo — [Signify IP](https://www.signifyip.com.au/blog/overcome-trademark-distinctiveness-objection); [Sprintlaw s 41](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/section-41-of-the-trade-marks-act-how-it-affects-registration/)
- Examiners look at the mark as a whole. Under s 41(4), a mark with some but not enough inherent distinctiveness can still be registered with evidence of use — [Sprintlaw s 41](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/section-41-of-the-trade-marks-act-what-it-means-for-your-trade-mark/); [Griffin Legal](https://griffinlegal.com.au/trade-marks-and-the-distinguishing-test/)
- IP Australia's manual also covers "fashionable" or new-terminology words that may be descriptive — [IP Australia Manual 22.14](https://manuals.ipaustralia.gov.au/trademark/14.-new-terminology-and--fashionable--words)
- A trade mark is protected only for the goods and services classes chosen — [IP Australia: classes](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/trade-marks/what-are-trade-marks/what-are-classes-of-goods-and-services) (via Sprintlaw summary)

### Inferences
- No source specifically assessed "care" for classes 44 and 45. In those classes "care", "healthcare" and "support services" plainly describe the services, so they are very likely non-distinctive on their own. Expect no exclusive right in those words even inside a registered composite mark.
- Recommended priority:
  1. **Word mark "AVENRIS"** first. It covers AVENRIS CARE, AVENRIS HEALTHCARE, AVENRIS TECHNOLOGIES and any logo styling. Infringement is judged on deceptive similarity, and the descriptive add-ons do not avoid it.
  2. **"AVENRIS CARE"** as a separate word mark only if budget allows. It adds little legal scope beyond AVENRIS.
  3. **A logo (composite) mark** once the logo is final, to protect the visual identity. A logo filing protects the mark as depicted and is narrower than a plain word mark. File the word mark first.
- One application covers one mark. Each extra mark (for example, the logo) means a new set of per-class fees.
- "AVENRIS" must still clear a search for similar earlier marks, for example similar-sounding names in classes 44 and 45. A coined word is strong on distinctiveness but can still conflict with earlier marks under s 44.

### Gaps
- No IP Australia examination decision specifically on "CARE" in classes 44 or 45 was found.
- Whether "Avenris" has any meaning in another language that could raise an issue was not checked.

## 3. Which Nice classes fit, and what care and NDIS providers typically file in

### Takeaway
For the care operating business, file in **Class 44** (medical, nursing, health care, hygienic care) and **Class 45** (personal and social services rendered by others to meet individuals' needs, which fits non-medical disability support, companionship and social escort). Optional additions: Class 35 (business management or franchising), Class 36 (financial or holding company services, only if they will be provided to others), and Classes 9 and 42 (software or SaaS, for "Avenris Technologies"). Classes 1-34 cover goods and 35-45 cover services. Protection is limited to the classes chosen.

### Cited Findings
- Class 44 covers medical services, veterinary services, and hygienic and beauty care for humans or animals. WIPO's Nice entries include "nursing home services", "rest home services" and "palliative care" — [WIPO Nice Class 44 (NCL listing)](https://nclpub.wipo.int/enfr/?basic_numbers=show&class_number=44&explanatory_notes=hide&lang=en&menulang=en&mode=flat&notion=&pagination=no&version=20200101); [Sprintlaw: 45 classes](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/trademark-classes-australia/)
- Class 45 covers legal and security services and personal and social services rendered by others to meet the needs of individuals, for example social escort services — [Canada CIPO class headings](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canadian-intellectual-property-office/en/trademarks/goods-and-services-manual-class-headings); [Sprintlaw: 45 classes](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/trademark-classes-australia/)
- Australia uses the 45 Nice classes: 1-34 are goods and 35-45 are services. A mark is protected only in the classes and for the goods or services chosen — [IP Australia: classes](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/trade-marks/what-are-trade-marks/what-are-classes-of-goods-and-services); [Progressive Legal](https://progressivelegal.com.au/what-are-the-trade-mark-classes-in-australia/)
- Non-medical companion care (meal prep, errands, transport, reminders) fits Class 45 "personal and social services". Hands-on personal care (bathing, dressing, toileting) may lean toward Class 44 hygienic or nursing care, so a mixed provider may file in both — [search-summary analysis of Nice headings plus industry descriptions; see Wikipedia: Nice Classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_(Nice)_Classification_of_Goods_and_Services)

### Inferences
- **Avenris Care / Care Support Services:** Class 44 (for example, nursing and home care, personal care and health care services) plus Class 45 (for example, personal support services for people with disabilities, companionship, social support). Using IP Australia's picklist terms is cheaper ($250 rather than $400 per class) and avoids wording objections.
- **Avenris Healthcare:** Class 44 is already covered by the AVENRIS word mark if filed in 44. Consider Class 41 if training or education services are planned, but that was not researched.
- **Avenris Technologies:** Class 9 (downloadable software and apps) and Class 42 (SaaS, software design and development). File when the plan firms up, or earlier if there is a real intention to use. Australian applications require an intention to use, and registered marks can be removed for non-use after a period of non-use. That period was not verified here.
- **Holding company:** Class 36 (financial services) or 35 (business management) is needed only if the holding company will provide those services to others in the market. Owning shares is not a "service", so many holding companies do not need 35 or 36.
- Practical minimum filing: AVENRIS word mark in Classes 44 and 45 (2 classes). Expanded filing: add 9 and 42 for tech, and possibly 35.

### Gaps
- No source surveyed the classes actual NDIS or home-care providers file in. A search of the ATMOSS / Australian Trade Mark Search register for marks owned by known NDIS providers would answer this but could not be run here.
- Exact IP Australia picklist wording for disability support services was not verified.

## 4. IP Australia fees, TM Headstart, timeline, opposition period, and attorney search cost

### Takeaway
The current standard application fee is **$250 per class** using the picklist, or **$400 per class** with free-text wording. This structure has applied since the October 2022 reform, and the 1 Oct 2024 fee changes left filing and renewal fees unchanged. There is no separate registration fee. Renewal is about $400 per class every 10 years. TM Headstart is a paid pre-assessment with a reply in about 5 business days, then conversion to a full application. Sources conflict on its current fees. A clean application takes about 7-8 months from filing to registration: examination within about 3-4 months, then a 2-month opposition period after acceptance is advertised. An attorney clearance search costs roughly $500-$1,050 + GST.

### Cited Findings
- Standard application: $250 per class with picklist, $400 per class without. Listed as "no change" for fees from 1 October 2024 — [Mirai Legal TM fee guide](https://mirailegal.au/guides/tm-fees); [LegalVision](https://legalvision.com.au/cost-of-trade-mark-registration); [Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/ip-australia-trade-mark-fees-explained/)
- The 1 October 2024 fee changes did not affect filing or renewal costs. They mainly changed opposition and non-use removal fees: $250 for each opposition ground above three, and a $350 fee to apply for non-use removal — [HWL Ebsworth (1 Oct 2024)](https://hwlebsworth.com.au/ip-australias-trade-mark-fee-changes-effective-from-1-october-2024/); [K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/Fee-Changes-for-Trade-Marks-and-Designs-in-Australia-Effective-1-October-2024-10-1-2024); [Macpherson Kelley](https://mk.com.au/fee-ling-the-change-ip-australia-fees-to-change/)
- The earlier fee review (2022) made average applications about a third cheaper. HWL footnotes cite $330 per class, which was the old free-text figure, and $400 renewal per class every 10 years — [HWL Ebsworth (fee review)](https://hwlebsworth.com.au/trade-mark-applications-cheaper-following-ip-australia-fee-review/). Conflict: one 2026 guide calls $330 the free-text fee, while most sources say $400. The Mirai guide shows $330 only in its 2020-2024 historical table — [search summary of Mirai Legal](https://mirailegal.au/guides/tm-fees)
- No 2026 change to IP Australia trade mark filing fees was found in search. Check IP Australia's price calculator — [IP Australia TM Checker options](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/trade-marks/search-existing-trade-marks/tm-checker/options)
- TM Headstart: an examiner assesses the request and responds within about 5 working days. Amendments must be made within 5 working days of the preliminary assessment. Headstart does not guarantee registration — [Sprintlaw TM Headstart 2026](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/trademark-headstart/); [Lavan](https://www.lavan.com.au/advice/intellectual_property_technology/trade_mark_registration); [Lawpath](https://lawpath.com.au/blog/what-is-ip-australias-tm-head-start-service/2)
- TM Headstart fees conflict:
  - Mirai Legal lists Part 1 at $200 per class.
  - Sources put the total at about $330 per class ($200 + $130 conversion), but the $130 Part 2 figure appears only in Mirai's 2020-2024 historical table.
  - Lavan cites an older $90 Part 2 fee.
  - Sources: [Mirai Legal](https://mirailegal.au/guides/tm-fees); [Cooper IP Headstart PDF (Aug 2025)](https://cooperip.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Australian-Trade-Mark-Application-Process-TM-Headstart.pdf); [Lavan](https://www.lavan.com.au/advice/intellectual_property_technology/trade_mark_registration)
- Timeline:
  - Examination is usually completed within about 4 months of filing (a 2026 firm guide says about 3-4 months) — [IPO reference guide](https://ipo.org/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/AGuidetoAustralianTrademarks.pdf); [Markster](https://www.markster.com.au/blog/understanding-the-australian-trade-mark-registration-process)
  - Opposition: anyone may file a notice of intention to oppose within 2 months of acceptance being published — [IP Australia Manual 47.1](http://manuals.ipaustralia.gov.au/trademark/1.-filing-a-notice-of-opposition)
  - IP Australia practice is not to publish acceptance until at least 5 months after the priority date. Registration can then happen in the period after opposition closes — [IP Australia Manual 39.4 Timing](https://manuals.ipaustralia.gov.au/trademark/4.-timing)
  - Earliest registration is about 7.5 months from filing (firm interpretation). A certificate issues about 2-3 weeks after the opposition period ends. Firms commonly report 7-8 months for a clean application — [Baxter IP](https://www.baxterip.com.au/trade-mark-registration-process); [Markster](https://www.markster.com.au/blog/understanding-the-australian-trade-mark-registration-process)
- Attorney search cost: The Loft Legal charges $500 + GST for a basic Australian word search and $850 + GST for a comprehensive search, with image searches at $700 or $1,050 + GST — [The Loft Legal fees](https://theloft.legal/fees). Wider ranges of $250 to $7,000 total for attorney-assisted registration are cited by [Lazarus Legal](https://lazaruslegal.com.au/how-much-does-a-trademark-cost/).

### Inferences
- **Indicative official cost, AVENRIS word mark:**
  - Classes 44 + 45 with picklist: 2 × $250 = **$500**.
  - Adding 9 + 42: another **$500** (total $1,000).
  - A logo mark in 44 + 45: another $500.
  - Attorney fees are extra. A clearance search is about $500-$1,050 + GST, and filing services typically add several hundred dollars or more per mark.
- **Value of an attorney search:** IP Australia's free tools (TM Checker, register search) mainly catch identical or near-identical marks. An attorney search covers phonetic and visual similarity across related classes, which matters for a coined word like "Avenris" that could sound like existing marks. For a brand intended to carry a group of companies, the roughly $500-$850 is cheap insurance compared with a rebrand.
- **Headstart vs direct filing:** Headstart is useful if the founder files without an attorney and wants examiner feedback before committing. If an attorney has already searched, a direct picklist application is simpler.
- **Timing:** the filing date is the priority date, so file AVENRIS as soon as the name is settled, ideally before public launch.

## 5. Restricted words and naming rules for business names; NDIS and aged care naming rules

### Takeaway
ASIC restricts certain words in business and company names. They are listed in Schedule 2 of the Business Names Registration (Availability of Names) Determination 2015, which also covers similar-meaning words, abbreviations and embedded words. ASIC also rejects "undesirable" names. No source showed "care", "healthcare", "support" or "technologies" on the restricted list, so "Avenris Care Support Services", "Avenris Healthcare" and "Avenris Technologies" appear registrable as business names subject to availability. The bigger risk for a care provider is misleading conduct: implying NDIS registration, NDIS approval or a government affiliation. The "NDIS" acronym and logo are NDIA trade marks and cannot be used in a name or branding without consent.

### Cited Findings
- Restricted words in business names are in Schedule 2 of the Business Names Registration (Availability of Names) Determination 2015. The restriction also covers similar-meaning words, abbreviations and embedded restricted words — [ASIC: Restricted words in business names](https://asic.gov.au/for-business/registering-a-business-name/before-you-register-a-business-name/business-name-availability/restricted-words-and-expressions-in-business-names)
- Examples:
  - Group 1 includes "charity" and "consumer".
  - Group 3 covers terms needing APRA consent, such as "bank", "ADI" and "building society".
  - Separate ministerial-consent guidelines exist, for example for "Ambulance" and "Fire Brigade".
  - Sources: [ASIC restricted words](https://asic.gov.au/for-business/registering-a-business-name/before-you-register-a-business-name/business-name-availability/restricted-words-and-expressions-in-business-names); [ASIC: Ambulance / Fire Brigade guidelines](https://asic.gov.au/for-business/registering-a-business-name/before-you-register-a-business-name/business-name-availability/guidelines-for-ministerial-consent/guidelines-for-the-use-in-business-names-of-the-word-or-expression-ambulance-and-fire-brigade)
- ASIC also has rules on "undesirable" names and on name availability (identical-name test) — [ASIC: undesirable business names](https://asic.gov.au/for-business/registering-a-business-name/before-you-register-a-business-name/business-name-availability/guidelines-for-ministerial-consent/undesirable-business-names/); [ASIC: availability rules](https://asic.gov.au/for-business/registering-a-business-name/before-you-register-a-business-name/business-name-availability/updated-rules-for-determining-the-availability-of-a-business-name); [ASIC: rules for acceptable names](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-business-and-companies/business-names/register-a-business-name/rules-for-acceptable-business-names/)
- Company names have a separate restricted-words page — [ASIC: Restricted words in company names](https://asic.gov.au/for-business/registering-a-company/steps-to-register-a-company/company-name-availability/restricted-words-in-company-and-body-corporate-names); [Lawpath](https://lawpath.com.au/blog/company-names-what-terms-are-restricted)
- NDIS branding rules:
  - The NDIS logo and acronym are registered trade marks, and no provider may use the NDIS logo without written NDIA consent. Only registered providers may use the "I/we heart NDIS" or "I/we support NDIS" logos, and only with the "Registered Provider" tagline — [NDIS logo guidelines](https://ndis.gov.au/contact/feedback-and-enquiries/ndis-logo-guidelines); [NDIS: Protecting the NDIS brand (PDF)](https://www.ndis.gov.au/print/pdf/node/29)
  - Providers must not suggest an affiliation with the NDIA or NDIS that does not exist, or falsely claim registration ("official NDIS provider"). That includes providers still going through registration. The NDIS does not "approve" goods or services, and the NDIA refers misleading claims to the ACCC — [NDIS: Cracking down on misleading 'NDIS-approved'](https://www.ndis.gov.au/news/11018-cracking-down-misleading-promotion-ndis-approved-products); [NDIS copyright/logo PDF](https://www.ndis.gov.au/print/pdf/node/25)
  - Secondary commentary: the NDIS Code of Conduct prohibits falsely claiming or implying registered-provider status through words or logos. The ACCC penalised Bedshed $39,600 over "NDIS approved" claims — [NDIS Growth (2026)](https://ndisgrowth.com.au/blog/ndis-marketing-rules/); [Brand Hero](https://brandhero.com.au/ndis-branding-guidelines-can-providers-use-the-ndis-logo/) (secondary, not verified against primary)
- Aged care: the Aged Care Act 2024 brought in a new provider registration model, a Code of Conduct and Quality Standards for registered providers, and compliance and penalty powers for the Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission — [Aged Care Act 2024 (legislation.gov.au)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2024A00104/latest/text); [myagedcare compliance](https://www.myagedcare.gov.au/compliance); [ACQSC associated providers](https://www.agedcarequality.gov.au/providers/guidance-associated-providers)

### Inferences
- Avoid "NDIS" in any business name, company name, domain or trade mark (for example, "Avenris NDIS Care"). Use "Registered NDIS Provider" wording only after registration is granted.
- "Healthcare" in "Avenris Healthcare" is not shown as ASIC-restricted. But calling a business "Healthcare" while delivering only non-clinical support could raise misleading-conduct risk under the Australian Consumer Law. Practitioner protected titles under the Health Practitioner Regulation National Law (for example "nurse", "physiotherapist") should not be implied unless registered practitioners provide those services. That legislation was not verified in this research.
- "Avenris Care Support Services" is low risk on restricted words. Ensure marketing does not imply NDIS registration or aged care registration before it is obtained.

### Gaps
- Could not view the full Schedule 2 list to confirm "care" and "health" are absent. Search found no evidence they are listed.
- No NDIS Commission rule found that regulates the provider's own name itself. The rules found concern logos, affiliation and misleading claims.
- Aged Care Act rules specific to naming or advertising were not found. AHPRA protected-title rules were not verified.

## 6. .com.au domain eligibility (auDA) and .au direct

### Takeaway
Currently, a .com.au requires an Australian commercial entity, most commonly an active ABN or ACN. The name must be an exact match, abbreviation or acronym of the registrant's name, or have a "close and substantial connection" to it. **Flag, recent change:** in September 2026 the auDA Board adopted a recommendation to delete the "close and substantial connection" limb. In future, .com.au eligibility would rest on a Match/Acronym test against the registrant's legal or registered business name or an Australian trade mark. Transition arrangements for existing domains are not yet settled. .au direct (for example, avenris.au) needs only an "Australian presence" and has no name-match rule. The 2022 priority-allocation window has closed.

### Cited Findings
- To register a com.au or net.au you must be an Australian commercial entity, most commonly with an active ABN or ACN. The business's physical address is not considered — [auDA: com.au and net.au rules](https://www.auda.org.au/au-domain-names/domain-name-help/comau-and-netau-rules-eligibility-and-allocation)
- Allocation criteria: an exact match, abbreviation or acronym of the registrant's name, or a close and substantial connection (a product sold, service provided, or event organised). Domain speculation or warehousing under the connection rule is not allowed — [auDA Guidelines on Open 2LDs](https://www.auda.org.au/au-domain-names/au-rules-and-policies/guidelines-interpretation-policy-rules-open-2lds-2012-05/); [auDA Licensing Rules](https://www.auda.org.au/au-domain-names/au-rules-and-policies/au-domain-administration-rules-licensing-2/)
- **September 2026:** the auDA Board adopted all ten recommendations of the .au Policy Advisory Panel, including deleting sub-para (f) of cl 2.4.4(2) ("close and substantial connection"). Eligibility would then rest on a Match/Acronym test against the registrant's legal or business name. Matching an Australian trade mark remains an alternative. One source says the decision is "approved in principle", and auDA says implementation planning will consider transition arrangements for existing registrations — [IP Twins (17 Sep 2026)](https://iptwins.com/2026/09/17/auda-to-require-tm-or-business-name-match-for-com-au/); [Domain Name Wire (8 Sep 2026)](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/09/08/auda-decision-could-impact-millions-of-com-au-domain-registrations/); [Mills IP](https://millsip.com.au/auda-close-substantial-connection-test-removed/); [Enom](https://www.enom.com/blog/what-audas-new-eligibility-rules-could-mean-for-your-domains/)

## 7. Is an international (Madrid) application worth it later?

### Takeaway
Madrid lets an Australian applicant extend an Australian application or registration (the "basic mark") to other member countries in one filing through IP Australia, paying WIPO fees in Swiss francs. It has to be based on the Australian mark and covers the same or narrower goods and services. For 5 years it depends on the basic mark: if the Australian mark is refused, cancelled or successfully attacked ("central attack"), the international registration falls with it. Filing within 6 months of the Australian filing date preserves the Australian priority date under the Paris Convention. That is general Madrid and Paris practice and was not directly re-verified in this search. For a care business serving Australians, Madrid is only worth it once there is a concrete plan for overseas markets, for example for "Avenris Technologies" software sold abroad.

### Cited Findings
- An Australian applicant needs a link to Australia (nationality, domicile or a real commercial establishment) and an Australian application or registration as the base — [Sprintlaw: Madrid Protocol](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/madrid-protocol-international-trade-marks/)
- Dependency and central attack: if the basic application or registration is refused, abandoned or cancelled within 5 years of the international registration date, the international registration is cancelled too. After 5 years it becomes independent — [Novagraaf](https://www.novagraaf.com/en/insights/international-trademarks-madrid-system-right-you/); [Perspire IP (2026)](https://www.perspireip.com/blog/madrid-protocol-trademark-registration-guide/); [INTA resolution on dependency (WIPO)](https://www.wipo.int/edocs/mdocs/madrid/en/mm_ld_wg_17/mm_ld_wg_17_inta.pdf)
- WIPO fees:
  - Basic fee CHF 653 (black and white) or CHF 903 (colour).
  - Complementary fee CHF 100 per class, or country-specific "individual fees".
  - Sources: [Perspire IP / Madrid fee guides](https://www.perspireip.com/blog/madrid-protocol-trademark-registration-guide/); [WIPO individual fees](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/madrid-system/fees/ind_taxes)
- Example estimate: one class designating US, EU, UK, China, Japan and Australia costs about CHF 3,500-4,500 in WIPO fees. This is a third-party estimate — [MarqVision](https://www.marqvision.com/blog/international-trademark-registration-fees)

### Inferences
- **Sequence:** secure the Australian AVENRIS mark first (classes 44 and 45, plus 9 and 42 if tech is planned). If overseas expansion such as NZ, UK or US for the tech arm is likely within 6 months of filing, a Madrid filing claiming Australian priority is efficient. Otherwise Madrid can be filed any time later off the Australian base, without the priority date.
- The 5-year central-attack risk argues for a clean, searched Australian base mark before relying on Madrid.
- Most Australian-only care providers would not need Madrid. The "Avenris Technologies" software arm is the most likely trigger.
- **New Zealand:** a direct NZ filing or a Madrid designation are both options. Comparative costs were not researched.

### Gaps
- IP Australia's handling or transmittal fee for Madrid applications was not found.
- Current WIPO fees were taken from secondary sources. Use WIPO's Madrid fee calculator.
- The 6-month Paris priority rule is standard but was not re-verified from a source in this session.
