Scene 1 — Cold Open (0:00–0:08)
Every term. Every subject. One teacher. Three subjects. Six forms.

Scene 2 — Stakes (0:08–0:16)
Sixteen schemes of work, written by hand, every single term. Each one takes
roughly three hours. Multiply that out, and one term alone swallows almost
fifty hours — for one teacher.

Scene 3 — Curriculum Turn (0:16–0:25)
Then Zimbabwe's curriculum changed overnight. The framework built for two
thousand fifteen through two thousand twenty-two was quietly retired.

Scene 4 — Introduce Gwara (0:25–0:34)
In its place: the two thousand twenty-four to two thousand thirty
Heritage-Based Curriculum Framework. This is Gwara — the same sixteen
schemes of work, generated in minutes, not hours.

Scene 5 — Trust Mechanism (0:34–0:43)
Nothing invented. Every scheme is grounded strictly in the real, official
ZIMSEC syllabus — checked automatically before a teacher ever sees it.

Scene 6 — Chain of Trust (0:43–0:53)
A teacher submits. A head reviews, backed by an automatic coverage check.
From there, district and provincial officials gain visibility too — across
every school, not just one classroom.

Scene 7 — Scale and Close (0:53–1:05)
Scale that up: one hundred forty-five thousand, six hundred and ten
teachers. Eleven thousand, seven hundred and ninety-three schools,
nationwide. Time back. Curriculum covered. Every term.
