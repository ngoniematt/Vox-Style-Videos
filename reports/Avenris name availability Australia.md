# Avenris looks clear, but verify before filing

**Indexed web search found no Australian company, business name, ABN, trade mark, NDIS registration or aged care listing for "Avenris", "Avenris Care", "Avenris Care Support Services", "Avenris Technologies" or "Avenris Healthcare", and no AVENRIS trade mark at any overseas office.** That makes the name look available. The main caveat is that this research could not query a single official register directly. The sandbox blocked ABN Lookup, ASIC, IP Australia, the NDIS Commission, My Aged Care, WIPO, USPTO, EUIPO, auDA and every WHOIS/RDAP service, so every "nothing found" here means "not found in indexed search results", not "confirmed clear". Indexed register pages are partial and often months out of date, and filings from 2025–26 are the least likely to show up. On what was found, conflict risk is **low for the care business (classes 44/45)** and **moderate for "Avenris Technologies" (classes 9/42)**. The tech risk comes from a live AI-services firm called **Avenris Solutions** that uses the identical word, and from a near-spelled software firm, **Avenrix**. The founder should get a professional clearance search, then file the **AVENRIS word mark in classes 44 and 45 straight away (about $500 in official fees)**, add 9 and 42 once the tech arm is real, and secure avenris.au alongside the avenris.com.au domain they already own. This report is research, not legal advice. Run the manual checks in the checklist below before you commit money or launch publicly.

## A trade mark, not an ASIC name, is what actually secures Avenris

Company names and business names registered with ASIC are administrative identifiers. They give **no exclusive right to the words** and do not stop others using similar names. Only a registered trade mark gives an enforceable, Australia-wide right for the listed goods and services ([IP Australia](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/trade-marks/what-are-trade-marks); [SLK Lawyers](https://www.slklawyers.com.au/business-name-vs-trade-mark-australia-why-asic-registration-does-not-protect-your-brand/)). A domain likewise gives only the domain. "Avenris Care Support Services" differs from the company name "Avenris Care Pty Ltd", so it **must be registered as a business name**. Trading under it unregistered breaches the Business Names Registration Act 2011 ([ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-business-and-companies/business-names/register-a-business-name); [Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/does-a-trading-name-need-to-be-registered/)).

File the **plain word mark AVENRIS**, not AVENRIS CARE. Invented words carry "very high" inherent distinctiveness under s 41 of the Trade Marks Act 1995 ([Signify IP](https://www.signifyip.com.au/blog/overcome-trademark-distinctiveness-objection); [IP Australia Manual](http://manuals.ipaustralia.gov.au/trademark/1.-registrability-under-section-41-of-the-trade-marks-act-1995)). "Care", "Healthcare", "Support Services" and "Technologies" describe the services and add no protection of their own. One word mark therefore covers every planned sub-brand and any logo styling. A logo mark can follow once the design is final. Protection extends only to the classes you choose ([IP Australia](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/trade-marks/what-are-trade-marks/what-are-classes-of-goods-and-services)), so the class plan matters:

| Class | Covers | For | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **44** | Medical, nursing, health and hygienic care, e.g. home and personal care, palliative care ([WIPO Nice](https://nclpub.wipo.int/enfr/?basic_numbers=show&class_number=44&explanatory_notes=hide&lang=en&menulang=en&mode=flat&notion=&pagination=no&version=20200101)) | Avenris Care, Avenris Healthcare | **Now** |
| **45** | Personal and social services meeting individuals' needs, e.g. disability support, companionship ([Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/trademark-classes-australia/)) | Avenris Care Support Services | **Now** |
| 9 | Downloadable software and apps | Avenris Technologies | When the tech plan is genuine (Australia requires an intention to use) |
| 42 | SaaS, software design and development | Avenris Technologies | Same as class 9. Filing early matters more here because of Avenris Solutions |
| 35 | Business management, franchising | Holding company | Only if providing these services to others |
| 36 | Financial services | Holding company | Only if providing financial services to others. Simply owning shares is not a service |

On cost, IP Australia charges **$250 per class using its picklist** or $400 per class with free-text wording. There is no separate registration fee, and renewal is about $400 per class every 10 years. The October 2024 fee changes left filing and renewal fees unchanged ([Mirai Legal](https://mirailegal.au/guides/tm-fees); [HWL Ebsworth](https://hwlebsworth.com.au/ip-australias-trade-mark-fee-changes-effective-from-1-october-2024/)). AVENRIS in classes 44 and 45 costs **$500 in official fees**, and adding 9 and 42 brings it to $1,000. An attorney clearance search costs about **$500 (basic) to $850 (comprehensive) + GST** at one published fee scale ([The Loft Legal](https://theloft.legal/fees)). That search is worth paying for: IP Australia's free tools mainly catch identical marks, and the AVENTIS and AVENIR questions are about phonetic and visual similarity. A clean application takes about **7–8 months** to register. Examination happens at about 3–4 months, then there is a 2-month opposition window after acceptance is advertised ([IP Australia Manual 47.1](http://manuals.ipaustralia.gov.au/trademark/1.-filing-a-notice-of-opposition); [Baxter IP](https://www.baxterip.com.au/trade-mark-registration-process)). TM Headstart gives examiner feedback within about 5 working days ([Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/trademark-headstart/)), but published fees for it conflict, so confirm on IP Australia's site. The 2026-27 ASIC fees from secondary guides are **$636 to register a Pty Ltd**, **$47 (1 year) or $108 (3 years) for a business name**, and $342 for the annual review ([Mirai Legal ASIC fees](https://mirailegal.au/guides/asic-fees); [AusBusinessRegister](https://ausbusinessregister.com.au/asic-fees-australia/)).

The cheapest sensible path costs roughly **$1,750–$2,200** before attorney filing fees: a clearance search, one company, a 3-year business name and a two-class trade mark.

On ownership, the usual structuring practice is for the future holding company "Avenris Pty Ltd" to own the mark and license it to the operating companies. If the holding company does not exist yet, file in Avenris Care Pty Ltd's name or the founder's name and assign later. Confirm this with an attorney or accountant. Madrid international filing is worth considering only when overseas plans are concrete, most likely for the tech arm. Filing within 6 months of the Australian filing keeps the Australian priority date. For the first 5 years, the international registration depends on the Australian mark surviving ([Novagraaf](https://www.novagraaf.com/en/insights/international-trademarks-madrid-system-right-you/)). WIPO's basic fee is CHF 653, plus per-country fees ([WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/madrid-system/fees/ind_taxes)).

## Your avenris.com.au needs a matching name under auDA's coming rules

Today a .com.au needs an Australian ABN or ACN and a domain that either matches the registrant's name or has a "close and substantial connection" to it ([auDA](https://www.auda.org.au/au-domain-names/domain-name-help/comau-and-netau-rules-eligibility-and-allocation)). In **September 2026 the auDA Board adopted a recommendation to delete the "close and substantial connection" limb**. Eligibility would then rest on matching the registrant's legal name, registered business name or an Australian trade mark. Transition rules for existing domains are not yet settled ([IP Twins](https://iptwins.com/2026/09/17/auda-to-require-tm-or-business-name-match-for-com-au/); [Domain Name Wire](https://domainnamewire.com/2026/09/08/auda-decision-could-impact-millions-of-com-au-domain-registrations/)). The founder already holds avenris.com.au. This research could not check which entity holds it. The safest way to keep it is to ensure the holder has an exact "Avenris" match, through any one of three routes: the holding company "Avenris Pty Ltd", a registered business name "Avenris", or the AVENRIS trade mark itself. The trade mark route gives a domain basis that survives restructures. avenriscare.com.au matches "Avenris Care Pty Ltd" under both the current and proposed rules. **avenris.au** (.au direct) needs only an Australian presence and should be registered now so nobody else can take it ([auDA](https://www.auda.org.au/blog/au-direct-webinar-priority-allocation-update)). Also check avenris.com. It has no indexed content, which does not prove it is available.

Two naming traps apply specifically to care providers. **"NDIS" must never appear** in a business name, domain or mark. The acronym and logo are NDIA trade marks, and the NDIA refers false "NDIS-approved" or registered-provider claims to the ACCC ([NDIS logo guidelines](https://ndis.gov.au/contact/feedback-and-enquiries/ndis-logo-guidelines); [NDIS](https://www.ndis.gov.au/news/11018-cracking-down-misleading-promotion-ndis-approved-products)). Separately, "Healthcare" is not shown on ASIC's restricted-words list ([ASIC](https://asic.gov.au/for-business/registering-a-business-name/before-you-register-a-business-name/business-name-availability/restricted-words-and-expressions-in-business-names)). Even so, using it for purely non-clinical support could raise misleading-conduct questions, and aged care registration under the Aged Care Act 2024 brings its own conduct obligations ([legislation.gov.au](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2024A00104/latest/text)).

## Conclusion

The search picture is that "Avenris" is uncontested in the sector the founder is entering first and contested in the sector they are entering later. In care, the coined word is strong and the nearest neighbours (Avera Care, the Avenues providers, a possible AVENTIS argument) are differences of degree that a clearance search can settle. In technology, someone already trades under the exact word, and the priority date that wins a later dispute comes from filing, not from intention. That reverses the instinct to "file 9 and 42 later". If Avenris Technologies is a genuine plan rather than a someday idea, filing those classes now, alongside 44 and 45, costs $500 and could prevent a future rebrand.

The second takeaway concerns the domain the founder already owns. auDA's September 2026 decision turns avenris.com.au from a domain you can keep through a loose business connection into one you keep through an exact name or trade mark match. That makes the AVENRIS trade mark more than brand protection: it is also the most durable legal basis for holding the domain. None of these conclusions replaces a live register search and advice from a registered trade mark attorney. Treat every "not found" in this report as a prompt to check, not a clearance.
